## Why

The queue's contract invests entirely in unattended **execution** — per-card approval freeze, branch isolation, caps, progress documents, an acceptance package. Nothing invests in **work selection**. Three consequences cost budget on every run: (1) cards that are duplicates of each other, or that share one root cause, are interviewed and executed independently, and cards not worth doing still consume a full deep-intake interview before anyone notices; (2) ordering is a three-level priority vocabulary plus FIFO, so the card whose outcome would prune the most downstream work is not run first; (3) a problem class solved once is re-interviewed from scratch the next time it appears. Parallelism addresses none of these — it compresses wall-clock time while leaving total work unchanged, and it adds merge, isolation and accounting cost. This change adds the missing selection layer.

## What Changes

- **Add — batch triage at enqueue, before the per-card intake.** One pass over the incoming card set produces equivalence groups, root-cause clusters, and suggested-kill cards. It runs *before* the per-card deep interview so eliminated work is never interviewed.
- **Add — certainty/difficulty assessment per card.** A coarse band plus named signals (reversibility, evidence already available, blast radius, dependency shape), recorded on the card and marked `factual` or `preference` per the existing discipline — never a numeric score (the repo prefers coarse bands over false precision).
- **Add — information-leverage ordering.** Dependency edges become a machine-readable card field (they currently exist only as prose in progress-document Notes). Among coupled cards, the card whose outcome can prune the most downstream cards is ordered first.
- **Add — approach reuse.** A card's frozen approach can be marked reusable; the next equivalent card's intake is seeded from it instead of re-interviewed. Carrier selection is delegated to `learn-and-improve` (recommend-only; shared-rule writes still require an explicit user request).
- **BREAKING — approval granularity.** The batch approval event that already exists on the Jira list-enqueue path becomes available on the general enqueue path, covering the triage outcome (merges, clusters, suggested kills) in one event. Per-card approval remains for cards not covered by a triage batch.
- **BREAKING — routing defaults.** Engine and stage-exit policy are defaulted from the assessment **when the assessment reaches the high-confidence band**; below that band the mandatory engine ticket remains mandatory. This flips the existing requirement's blanket "no default value" rule.
- **BREAKING — ordering vocabulary.** The assessment becomes a first-class ordering dimension; FIFO within a priority level is retained only as the final tie-break.
- **Guardrails (design constraints, not optional).** Automatic kill applies only to mechanically provable cases (equivalence, already-covered) and is recorded reversibly — cards are archived, never deleted, each pointing at its covering report. Value-judgment "not worth doing" suggestions stay human-confirmed. Every assessment-driven default is confidence-gated and independently overridable.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `goal-queue-triage`: enqueue-time batch triage (equivalence merge, root-cause clustering, kill), per-card certainty/difficulty assessment, and information-leverage ordering of coupled cards.
- `goal-queue-reuse`: marking a frozen approach reusable and seeding a new equivalent card's intake from it, with carrier selection delegated to `learn-and-improve`.

### Modified Capabilities

- `goal-queue`: card fields gain the assessment and dependency-edge fields; approval granularity gains the batch path; engine/policy routing becomes confidence-gated-defaulted; the ordering vocabulary gains the assessment dimension; the relationship pass is re-anchored so triage happens before intake rather than only at dispatch.
